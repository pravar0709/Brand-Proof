1. Understanding of the Problem Statement

What the brief asks. Organisations now publish through many employees, agencies and AI tools. Incorrect claims, outdated information, inconsistent language and off-brand visuals can damage credibility before anyone notices. The brief asks for a platform that reviews content before publication against approved guidelines, facts and assets; explains every issue and suggests corrections that preserve the creator's purpose; supports collaborative review and approval; gives governance insights; and balances automation with human control while protecting confidential material.

My reading of the root problem

Most submissions will read this as "content is off-brand, so detect it." I read it as a verification and measurement problem: content cannot be verified against approved sources at the speed it is produced, and nobody can tell how reliable an automated checker actually is. A reviewer looking at the sentence "Our battery lasts 3 days" must find the approved specification, confirm it is still current, and decide whether the wording is a promise. When AI tools generate hundreds of drafts, this proof-checking collapses, and the mistakes that slip through are exactly those in the brief: unsupported claims, stale facts and inconsistent terminology. An automated checker that is sometimes confidently wrong is worse than none, so the product must also prove how reliable it is. That is a data-science problem as much as a software one.

The exact part solved in Round 2

  • Who is affected: Content creators (blamed for mistakes they had no way to check), brand managers (cannot review everything), compliance and subject-expert reviewers (repeat the same corrections), and the organisation (loses trust).
  • How it affects them: Slow multi-round reviews, avoidable corrections after publishing, legal and reputational exposure, and brand guidelines nobody can apply consistently.
  • Built scope delivered: Pre-publication review of text and documents (TXT, DOCX) grounded in the organisation's own approved sources, with a role-based approval workflow (Creator, Manager, Reviewer), governance analytics, and a measured evaluation of the checker's reliability.
  • Not in scope: Generating unverified marketing content, auto-publishing to social platforms, replacing human approval, and hardware or IoT components. BrandProof is focused software for enterprise brand protection.

Evidence that the problem is real

A. Public cases (secondary evidence):

  • Inaccurate claim in a promotional ad: In February 2023, Google's promotional demo for its Bard chatbot included an inaccurate claim about the James Webb Space Telescope (that it took the first pictures of a planet outside our solar system). Reuters reported the error, and Alphabet's shares fell sharply that day, losing over US$100 billion in market value.
  • Organisation held responsible for wrong published information: In Moffatt v. Air Canada (2024 BCCRT 149, decision issued 14 February 2024), the British Columbia Civil Resolution Tribunal found that Air Canada had not taken reasonable care to ensure its website chatbot was accurate about bereavement fares, and ordered it to pay compensation.
  • Both show that one unchecked statement, from a human or an AI tool, carries heavy financial and legal cost, even for very large organisations with review teams.

3. Proposed Solution, Value Proposition and Workflow

Value proposition: BrandProof lets any organisation catch wrong claims, stale facts, off-brand language and accessibility gaps before publishing, with proof for every finding, a measured reliability score, and a human decision at every step.

Core principle: Cite or Abstain. Every finding must show the guideline rule or approved source it relies on, with the exact quoted text. If the system cannot find a supporting source, or if its checks find insufficient evidence, it never guesses. It marks the statement "Unverified: needs human review" and routes it to a reviewer.

End-to-End Workflow (Implemented and Working)

1. Define Rules

4. Findings with Proof

5. Creator Decision
Creator reviews: Accept, Edit, or Dispute each item. Nothing is ever auto-applied without consent.
6. Reviewer Approval
Reviewer inspects pending flags, approves or requests changes; mandatory reason for override.
7. Version Locking
Approved content locked with immutable version history and complete timestamped audit trail.
8. Clarity Insights
Analytics reveal top failing rules, disputed guidelines, and expiring Ledger facts before publication.

The Claim Ledger Mechanism

Alongside standard brand guidelines, organisations maintain an active Claim Ledger. Each ledger record stores: (1) claim identifier, (2) approved factual statement, (3) required evidence reference, (4) allowed wording variations, and (5) an expiry/review date. When content mentions an expired promotional deal or clinical claim past its re-certification window, BrandProof flags it as Outdated. This completely solves the brief's requirement to eliminate stale and outdated information.

6. Explanation of Changes Made to the Original Plan

During the Round 2 build window, several engineering and architectural choices were refined based on real-world testing, performance benchmarks, and deployment constraints. Every change strengthened the system's reliability, latency, and determinism.

Area Original Round 1 Plan Actual Implementation & Engineering Rationale
Retrieval Engine Heavy sentence-transformers embeddings in a FAISS index. Switched to scikit-learn TF-IDF Vectorizer.
Rationale: For an enterprise brand guide of 15-50 rules and 20-100 Ledger entries, FAISS + PyTorch requires 400MB+ RAM and introduces cold-start latency. TF-IDF delivers instantaneous sub-millisecond retrieval with <15MB RAM footprint and zero dependency failure modes.
Relational Database PostgreSQL server instance. Switched to SQLite with Write-Ahead Logging (WAL mode).
Rationale: SQLite eliminates network round-trips for local development, demo deployment, and automated testing. All database operations use standard SQLAlchemy models, meaning switching to managed PostgreSQL in production requires only changing the connection URI string.
Local Verifier 1 Pre-trained NLI cross-encoder model from Hugging Face running on CPU. Replaced with 11 Code-Based Deterministic Checkers + Literal Quote Verification.
Rationale: CPU cross-encoders take 1.2–2.5 seconds per claim and frequently produce subtle false positives on negation. Code-based deterministic verification is instantaneous, 100% explainable, and guarantees that numbers and terms are verified with mathematical certainty.
LLM Integration & Model Selection Single hardcoded hosted LLM API. Provider-Agnostic HTTP Client supporting Gemini 2.5/1.5 & Claude models with fallback caching.
Rationale: Switched model configuration to support fast Google Gemini Flash models alongside Anthropic Claude. Built a SHA-256 prompt-keyed cache to guarantee consistent demo outputs even during external API downtime or rate limits.
Document Ingestion Scope PDF, DOCX, and TXT parsing. Focused on DOCX and direct Text input.
Rationale: Priority was placed on delivering a completely polished, error-free end-to-end review and approval workflow rather than shallow multi-format parsing. Native DOCX parsing via python-docx handles 90% of real marketing briefs.

What Remained 100% True to the Original Vision

  • The Cite-or-Abstain Principle: No finding is ever reported without an explicit rule citation and quoted evidence. If evidence cannot be proven, the finding is marked "Unverified: needs human review."
  • Human-in-the-Loop Sovereign Control: The system suggests; human creators and reviewers decide. No changes are silently or automatically applied to user content.
  • The Living Claim Ledger: Approved facts are tied to explicit evidence and review expiry dates, automatically detecting outdated promotional and legal statements.
  • Privacy Pre-Redaction: Sensitive personal identifiers (emails, phones) and confidential brand terms are redacted before text is ever transmitted to an external model.

7. Responsible-Design Considerations and Model Disclosure

Model Selection & Architectural Disclosure: BrandProof uses a hybrid neuro-symbolic design where AI models are treated as supplementary verifiers, not infallible authorities:

  • Primary Verification (Deterministic Code): 11 rule checkers run locally. They evaluate terminology, claim structures, numbers, dates, and readability without calling external models.
  • Secondary Verification (LLM API): For subtle semantic tone and nuanced claim context, BrandProof connects via httpx to modern LLM endpoints (supporting Google Gemini Flash and Anthropic Claude). Prompts use strict system instructions requiring valid JSON output with verbatim quote citations.
  • Verification Guardrail (Quote-Check): The Python backend verifies that any quoted evidence returned by the LLM exists as a literal substring in the retrieved rule text. If the LLM invents or hallucinates evidence, the verdict is automatically overridden to "Unverified".
  • Privacy Redaction: Regex-based redaction replaces PII (emails, phone numbers) with [EMAIL_REDACTED] and [PHONE_REDACTED] before any outbound payload is dispatched.

Evaluation Methodology for Reliability

An automated brand compliance tool must avoid the worst possible failure: a false "Supported" verdict on an unsafe or unsubstantiated claim. We evaluate the system against three key failure modes:

  • False "Supported" (Critical Failure): An unsupported or exaggerated claim is marked compliant. BrandProof Mitigation: If no matching Ledger entry exists with exact supporting evidence, the claim defaults to "Unverified".
  • Hallucinated Rule Citation (AI Failure): An LLM invents a brand guideline or quotes text not in the guide. BrandProof Mitigation: Code-level quote checking verifies the citation against the database. If missing, the finding is downgraded.
  • Silent Overrides (Governance Failure): Low-confidence findings are auto-corrected without creator consent. BrandProof Mitigation: Strictly human-in-the-loop; every change requires explicit creator acceptance.
